# The three-visit habit (the red napkin)

**The story.** A restaurant asks every guest: "Have you been here before?" A first-timer gets a **red napkin**;
everyone else gets white. Now the whole staff can see who is new without a word. The first-timer came in for a free
rib dinner, traded for their email. At the end of the meal the manager walks over, asks how it was, and writes on the
back of a business card: *free chicken sandwich*. They come back. After visit two the manager asks about the
chicken and writes: *free cheesecake, because we have the best cheesecake.* After the third visit there is a good
chance of a fourth.

**A habit is three visits. Engineer the first three.**

## The five moves

1. **Spot the first-timer (the red napkin).** Mark a new customer the moment they book or walk in, so everyone who
   touches them (staff, the phone, the emails, the chat assistant) can see it.
2. **A free, specific reason for visit one,** traded for an email or phone number. A real item with a name.
   Never "10% off".
3. **A person closes the visit.** "How was it?", asked by name, by a human or in the owner's voice. Never a survey link.
4. **A hand-written, specific reason for the next visit.** One named item, and a **different** one each time, so each
   visit shows them something else you are best at. Ribs, then chicken, then cheesecake.
5. **Count to three.** Track visit 1, 2 and 3 per customer. The number that matters: of the first-timers, how many reach
   visit three. After three, stop the gifts. They are regulars now. Recognise them by name instead.

## The rules for the gift

- Always specific, always something you are proud of.
- Handed over at the **end** of a good experience, never as an ad.
- Has the customer's first name on it.
- Expires (12 months is a good default).
- Costs you little and feels like a lot.

## Tourists do not come back next Tuesday

For a tourism business a "visit" is a **purchase**, not a trip. Three purchases can happen inside one trip, across
partner businesses, or on the next trip.

| Business | Visit 1 (free reason) | Card after visit 1 | Card after visit 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bike or scooter rental | free drop-off at your hotel | "Tomorrow: free snorkel set with your ride" | "Free extra hour, any day this trip" |
| Café | free pastry with the first coffee | "Your next cold brew is on us" | "Free bag of our house beans" |
| Hair salon | free deep-conditioning with the first cut | "Free brow shape next visit" | "Bring a friend, their wash is free" |
| Tour operator | the tour | "Free bike day after your tour" | "Bring someone free on a weekday" |
| Vacation rental | the stay | "First hour of bikes free on arrival" | "Late checkout next stay, book direct" |

These are starting points. The owner picks the real gifts.

## How to build it with what most businesses already have

1. **Visit number on every booking or receipt.** Match by email or phone. Show a badge for visit 1 and visit 2.
2. **The card in the post-visit message.** The thank-you or review-request email, sent a day or three after the visit,
   carries the card: visit 1 gets gift A, visit 2 gets gift B. Each is a single-use code with the first name and an
   expiry, for example `GIFT-ANA-4821`.
3. **Claim it at the next purchase.** When the same email or phone books again, show staff "Hand over: <gift>".
   A person hands it over. No price changes needed.
4. **One number on the owner's dashboard:** first-timers this month, how many reached 2, how many reached 3.

## What to produce when the user asks

- The two cards (gift after visit 1, gift after visit 2) for their business, specific and named.
- The post-visit message that carries the card, in their voice, under 80 words.
- Where the visit number will live (their booking tool, POS or a spreadsheet) and how staff will see it.
- The one number to watch.
