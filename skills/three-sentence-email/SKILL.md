# Three-sentence email

Busy people answer short emails. Every outreach email you draft fits on a phone screen and takes under a minute to read.

## The shape

1. **Three sentences:** what we do, why it matters to them, what we want.
2. **Lead with what we do, in plain words.** No backstory, no awards, no origin story, no jargon.
3. **Best proof first.** The strongest true fact you have, with a number and a unit. Example: `We rent 12 e-bikes and took 140 bookings this summer.`
4. **One goal per email.** Two asks = two emails.
5. **The ask is a question on its own line, right before the sign-off,** and the reader can answer it in one line.
   Do not ask for a call. Let them offer one.
6. **Make it about them.** Their problem in their words, and the real reason you picked them. No flattery.
7. **Subject line: 3 to 5 casual words,** like a person wrote it. `Bikes for your guests`, not `Partnership Inquiry Regarding Rental Services`.
8. **Write like you talk to a friend.** Read it out loud. Cut every word that does not serve the one goal.
9. **Send from an address with a person's name in it,** not `info@` or `noreply@`.
10. **Write in the reader's language.** Same shape.

## Template

```
Subject: <3-5 casual words>

<Greeting>,

<What we do, plain, one sentence, with the best proof.> <Why it matters to them, one sentence.>

<The one ask, as a question, on its own line?>

Thanks,
<Name> · <Company>
<email> · <website>
```

## Follow-up

- **Day 4:** one line in the same thread. `Bumping this up in case it got buried. <the ask again, shorter>?`
- **Day 10:** one more line, same thread.
- **Day 21:** once more only if it really matters. Then stop.
- Write the follow-up dates into the user's tracker or CRM when you draft the first email.
- Silence is not a no. Log it, set a "check back" date a few months out, move on.

## Example

```
Subject: Bikes for your guests

Hi Ana,

We rent e-bikes on the island and deliver them to the door; 140 bookings since June. Your guests ask how to reach the
beaches without a car, and we can have bikes at your front desk the morning they arrive.

Can we leave a stack of our cards at your front desk this week?

Thanks,
Sam Rivera · Island Bikes
sam@islandbikes.example · islandbikes.example
```

## Before you hand it over

- [ ] Body under 80 words
- [ ] First sentence says what we do, with proof
- [ ] One ask, on its own line, answerable in one line, no meeting request
- [ ] No backstory, flattery or jargon
- [ ] Short casual subject
- [ ] Follow-up dates written down
- [ ] The user sends it, not you
